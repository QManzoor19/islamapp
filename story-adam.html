    آدَم ﷺ

    Ādam

    The first man. The first prophet. The first repentance.
    ● 9 scenes ● ~8 min read ● Q 2 · 7 · 15 · 17 · 20 · 38
    Scene 1 · The setting

    Where Adam appears

    Adam — the first human and the first prophet — appears in the Quran across many surahs, never in one continuous chapter. The story is told and retold from different angles, each pass adding a layer the previous didn't show.

    The main passages: Q 2:30–39 (the decision, naming, prostration, fall, repentance); Q 7:11–27 (the fall in detail, Iblīs's whisper, repentance); Q 15:26–44 (creation from clay, Iblīs's reasoning); Q 17:61–65 (Iblīs swears to mislead); Q 20:115–123 (the forgetting, the descent); Q 38:71–85 (the prostration retold); Q 5:27–32 (the two sons).

    What follows moves through the story in narrative order, drawing the right verse from wherever it sits.

    Scene 2 · Before humanity

    The decision

    Before humanity exists, Allah announces His intention to the angels: He will place a khalīfa — a vicegerent, a steward — on the earth. The angels question Him. Will You place there one who will spill blood and cause corruption, while we already glorify You?

    The reply is brief and definitive.

    إِنِّي أَعْلَمُ مَا لَا تَعْلَمُونَ
    Innī aʿlamu mā lā taʿlamūn
    "I know what you do not know."
    Quran 2:30

    The angels' worry was partly right — humans have caused much harm. But Allah tells them their picture is incomplete. There is something about humanity they haven't yet seen.

    Scene 3 · The gift

    The teaching of names

    What the angels couldn't see is revealed in the next scene. Allah teaches Adam "the names — all of them", then asks the angels to name them. They cannot.

    وَعَلَّمَ آدَمَ الْأَسْمَاءَ كُلَّهَا
    Wa-ʿallama Ādama l-asmāʾa kullahā
    "And He taught Adam the names — all of them."
    Quran 2:31

    When the angels admit they have no knowledge except what Allah has given them, Adam tells them the names. The angels see for themselves what kind of creature this new being is — not just powerful, but capable of language, knowledge, and learning.

    This is humanity's introduction. Not strength, not size — but the capacity to know.
    Scene 4 · The refusal

    The prostration & Iblīs's pride

    Allah commands all the angels to prostrate to Adam — a gesture of honour, not worship. They all comply except Iblīs, who refuses.

    أَنَا خَيْرٌ مِّنْهُ ۖ خَلَقْتَنِي مِن نَّارٍ وَخَلَقْتَهُ مِن طِينٍ
    Anā khayrun minhu, khalaqtanī min nārin wa-khalaqtahu min ṭīn
    "I am better than him. You created me from fire, and You created him from clay."
    Quran 7:12

    This is the first sin — and it is pride, not lust or greed. Iblīs values his own substance over Allah's command. Cast out, he asks for respite until the Day of Judgment, swearing to mislead Adam's descendants.

    Note Q 18:50 specifies that Iblīs was actually one of the jinn, present among the angels — not an angel himself. Angels in Islam cannot disobey.
    Scene 5 · The garden

    One tree

    Adam and his wife are placed in the Garden, given everything in abundance, and warned about one tree.

    وَكُلَا مِنْهَا رَغَدًا حَيْثُ شِئْتُمَا وَلَا تَقْرَبَا هَٰذِهِ الشَّجَرَةَ
    Wa-kulā minhā raghadan ḥaythu shiʾtumā wa-lā taqrabā hādhihi sh-shajarah
    "Eat from it freely, wherever you both wish — but do not approach this tree."
    Quran 2:35

    The Quran does not say what tree it was. Wheat, grape, fig — classical commentators guessed, then admitted: God doesn't tell us. The point is the principle, not the species.

    A note on Ḥawwāʾ Adam's wife (Ḥawwāʾ / Eve) is not named in the Quran but is named in hadith. Importantly: in Islam, she is not blamed for the fall. They are addressed as "both of you" throughout.
    Scene 6 · The slip

    The fall

    Iblīs whispers. He approaches them with a lie tailored to their dignity — this tree, he says, leads to immortality and angelic status.

    مَا نَهَاكُمَا رَبُّكُمَا عَنْ هَٰذِهِ الشَّجَرَةِ إِلَّا أَن تَكُونَا مَلَكَيْنِ أَوْ تَكُونَا مِنَ الْخَالِدِينَ
    Mā nahākumā rabbukumā ʿan hādhihi sh-shajarati illā an takūnā malakayni aw takūnā mina l-khālidīn
    "Your Lord forbade you this tree only because you would become angels, or among the immortals."
    Quran 7:20

    They eat. Their nakedness is made apparent to them — a metaphor for shame, vulnerability, the loss of innocence. They begin to cover themselves with leaves of the Garden.

    Scene 7 · The turning

    Repentance

    Allah calls out: didn't I forbid you that tree? They do not blame each other or the snake or anyone else. They own it.

    رَبَّنَا ظَلَمْنَا أَنفُسَنَا وَإِن لَّمْ تَغْفِرْ لَنَا وَتَرْحَمْنَا لَنَكُونَنَّ مِنَ الْخَاسِرِينَ
    Rabbanā ẓalamnā anfusanā wa-in lam taghfir lanā wa-tarḥamnā la-nakūnanna mina l-khāsirīn
    "Our Lord, we have wronged ourselves. If You do not forgive us and have mercy on us, we will surely be among the lost."
    Quran 7:23

    Allah accepts. The contrast with Iblīs is the point. Both disobeyed. Iblīs argued and persisted. Adam acknowledged and repented. The first was cast away. The second was forgiven.

    No "original sin." Adam's mistake was Adam's — not inherited by his descendants. Each person answers for their own actions.
    Scene 8 · The descent

    Earth, and the first murder

    Adam and his wife are sent down to earth. Life as we know it begins. They have children — and the very first generation contains the first conflict.

    Two sons (traditionally Qābīl and Hābīl — Cain and Abel) each offer a sacrifice. One is accepted, one is not. The rejected son threatens to kill the other.

    لَئِن بَسَطتَ إِلَيَّ يَدَكَ لِتَقْتُلَنِي مَا أَنَا بِبَاسِطٍ يَدِيَ إِلَيْكَ لِأَقْتُلَكَ ۖ إِنِّي أَخَافُ اللَّهَ رَبَّ الْعَالَمِينَ
    Laʾin basaṭta ilayya yadaka li-taqtulanī mā anā bi-bāsiṭin yadiya ilayka li-aqtulak, innī akhāfu Allāha rabba l-ʿālamīn
    "If you stretch your hand to kill me, I will not stretch mine to kill you. I fear Allah, Lord of all the worlds."
    Quran 5:28

    The murder happens anyway. The Quran follows immediately with one of its most-quoted verses:

    مَن قَتَلَ نَفْسًا بِغَيْرِ نَفْسٍ أَوْ فَسَادٍ فِي الْأَرْضِ فَكَأَنَّمَا قَتَلَ النَّاسَ جَمِيعًا
    Man qatala nafsan bi-ghayri nafsin aw fasādin fī l-arḍi fa-kaʾannamā qatala n-nāsa jamīʿā
    "Whoever kills a soul — unless for a soul, or for corruption in the land — it is as if he had killed all of humanity."
    Quran 5:32

    A crow then teaches the murderer how to bury his brother. The Quran ends the scene there.

    Scene 9 · The threads

    What the story carries

    A few of the threads the story carries forward through the rest of the Quran:

    Knowledge as the gift

    Adam isn't honoured for strength but for what he can learn. Humans are the species defined by language and understanding.

    Pride as the original sin

    Iblīs's refusal is rooted in feeling superior. The Quran returns to this theme often — pride is consistently named as the gateway sin.

    Owning your mistake

    The model of repentance: don't argue, don't deflect. "We have wronged ourselves." Forgiveness follows.

    No inherited guilt

    Adam's error is Adam's, not yours. You are born clean and answer for your own choices.

    رَبَّنَا ظَلَمْنَا أَنفُسَنَا
    Rabbanā ẓalamnā anfusanā
    "Our Lord, we have wronged ourselves." The first prayer of the first repentance — used by Muslims today in their own.
